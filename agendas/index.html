Foreign Relations & International Affairs

Three Initial Agendas

The following three priorities are proposed as the initial international-affairs agenda for the new office: historical accountability, a structured global Telangana network, and international policy and knowledge partnerships.

  • 01Historical Accountability and Formal British Acknowledgement
  • 02Telangana Students, Professionals and Visa Mobility
  • 03Fair Compensation and Value for Telangana's Technology Talent
01

Historical Accountability and Formal British Acknowledgement

The proposed first international agenda is to place the history and continuing legacy of British colonial rule in India on the international policy and historical-dialogue agenda, with a specific objective of seeking a formal acknowledgement and, where appropriate, an apology from the United Kingdom for documented harms associated with colonial rule.

The office should develop a rigorously sourced historical dossier covering colonial administration, taxation, trade policy, economic extraction, changes to Indian industries and agriculture, famines and other documented social and economic consequences. The dossier should distinguish established historical evidence, scholarly estimates and contested interpretations.

For the report's historical framing, Cambridge's Economic History of Modern South Asia uses 1757–1947 as its period for colonial India. Accordingly, the report should avoid treating the phrase "250 years of looting" as a precise historical period without qualification. A more defensible formulation is that British political and economic control developed over more than two centuries, beginning in the eighteenth century and ending with independence in 1947.

Relevant scholarship documents major changes in Indian trade, taxation, agriculture, industry and economic structure during colonial rule, while scholarly debate continues over the scale and mechanisms of economic extraction. Any quantified estimate of wealth transferred should therefore be attributed to its source rather than presented as an uncontested fact.

Proposed First Deliverable

  1. A documented colonial-history dossier with a source bibliography.
  2. A chronology from eighteenth-century Company rule through Crown rule and independence in 1947.
  3. A review of economic, social, cultural and heritage consequences.
  4. A comparative study of international apologies and historical-justice initiatives.
  5. A diplomatic options paper identifying possible forms of acknowledgement or apology.

Proposed Institutional Position

The proposed office could present the issue as a historical-accountability initiative: seeking recognition of documented colonial harms while maintaining a contemporary relationship with the United Kingdom based on dialogue, research, education and international cooperation.

02

Telangana Students, Professionals and Visa Mobility

A second priority should be to represent the concerns of Telangana students, skilled professionals and families with strong educational, professional and community ties to the United States.

The office could establish a "Telangana–U.S. Student and Professional Mobility Initiative" to document recurring concerns and take them through appropriate U.S. diplomatic, governmental and institutional channels.

The objective would be to explore more predictable and efficient visa appointment and processing systems, greater transparency around timelines and administrative processing, improved mechanisms for time-sensitive student cases, and clearer processes for professionals and families, while respecting U.S. immigration, security and legal requirements.

The U.S. Department of State publishes location- and category-specific visa wait-time information and states that wait times can vary by location, season and visa category. Student applicants remain subject to applicable eligibility and screening requirements. The office should therefore focus on reducing avoidable procedural uncertainty rather than seeking exemption from legitimate legal requirements.

Priority Areas

Student visa appointment availability and predictability
Time-sensitive cases where admission deadlines are approaching
Transparency around processing and administrative delays
Professional mobility and employer-related visa processes
Dependent and family mobility issues
Structured communication between universities, employers, applicants and relevant authorities

Proposed First Deliverable

Within the first 100 days, the office could publish a "Telangana–United States Student & Professional Mobility Report" documenting student mobility, professional mobility, recurring visa concerns, processing challenges, and feedback from Telangana-origin communities.

03

Fair Compensation and Value for Telangana's Technology Talent

The third agenda should address the value created by Indian technology professionals for U.S. and other multinational companies operating in India. The office could initiate structured discussions with major international companies on competitive compensation, career progression, skills development and the expansion of higher-value technology functions in Telangana.

India's technology industry has become a major component of the global technology ecosystem. NASSCOM's recent industry analysis places the sector at roughly $315 billion in FY26 and approximately six million direct technology-sector employees, while its GCC research documents the continued expansion of multinational technology operations in India.

The proposed office should not attempt to dictate individual employment contracts or replace labour law. Instead, it should create a structured international-business dialogue encouraging transparent, competitive and merit-based compensation and greater recognition of the value generated by Indian teams.

From Cost Advantage
to Value Advantage

  • Competitive compensation based on role, responsibility, skills, experience and market benchmarks.
  • Equal opportunity for advancement into global leadership, product, research and engineering roles.
  • Greater placement of high-value work and decision-making in Telangana and India.
  • Investment in advanced skills, research and employee development.
  • Transparent, appropriately anonymised compensation and career data for policy research.

Telangana–U.S. Technology Talent Dialogue

The office could establish an annual dialogue bringing together major U.S. technology companies, Global Capability Centres, technology professionals, industry associations, universities, entrepreneurs, researchers and relevant institutional stakeholders.

A voluntary "Telangana Global Technology Talent Compact" could also be explored, covering principles such as competitive compensation, transparent career progression, equal opportunity, investment in skills, expansion of high-value functions, research opportunities and leadership development.

Proposed First Deliverable

A "Global Technology Compensation & Opportunity Report — Telangana" could examine compensation by discipline and seniority, skills in demand, career progression, high-value technology functions, research and engineering opportunities, retention and international investment trends, using independent and appropriately anonymised data.
